Florida · State guide

Florida registers the room, not just the surgeon.

Four things the state will tell you for free, before you pay anyone a deposit: whether the operating room is registered, whether the licence is clean, which board actually certified the surgeon, and who is giving you anaesthesia. It takes about twenty minutes.

The room

An unregistered operating room is the cheapest red flag to find.

Under sections 458.328 and 459.0138 of the Florida Statutes, an office where a physician performs any Level II or Level III office surgery, or liposuction that removes more than 1,000 cubic centimetres of supernatant fat, has to be registered with the Florida Department of Health. The only exception is a facility already licensed as a hospital or ambulatory surgical centre under chapter 390 or 395.

Registration is not a rubber stamp. A registered office surgery setting has to be inspected by the Department or accredited by a national accrediting organisation, and since 2024 operating outside a registered setting carries a fine of $5,000 per incident.

So the question is short. Is this address a registered office surgery setting, or a licensed hospital or surgical centre? Ask it before anything else, and read the answer against the Board of Medicine's own office surgery registration program.

The licence

The licence lookup is free, and the name on it has to match your consent form.

Florida publishes every health care practitioner licence it issues, with status and discipline, at the Department of Health's own licence verification service. Search the exact name printed on the consent form, not the name on the Instagram account.

Read three things. The licence is current. The profession on it is the one you think you are buying. And the discipline section is empty, or if it is not, you have read what is there and decided for yourself.

The certification

Board certified says nothing until you ask which board.

There is no single body called the board. Certifications differ in what training they required and which procedures they cover, and a surgeon can be genuinely certified by one board and still be operating outside what that certification covers.

Two questions close the gap. Which board certified you, and does that certification cover this operation? Then check the certifying board's own lookup rather than the practice's website, because the website is marketing and the lookup is the record.

The anaesthesia

Ask who gives your anaesthesia and whether they stay in the room.

Anaesthesia is usually the part of the plan nobody volunteers. Ask for the name, the licence, and whether that person stays with you for the whole procedure or sets you up and leaves. Get it in writing before a deposit, then check the licence on the same state lookup you used for the surgeon.

Florida's own rules treat this as load bearing. Level II and Level III office surgery requires that at least one assistant to the surgeon holds current Basic Life Support certification, and the levels themselves are defined in rule 64B8-9.009 of the Florida Administrative Code.

Before the deposit

Seven things to get in writing while you still have leverage.

A deposit is the moment your options narrow. Before it goes across, ask for these in writing, in one email, so the answers exist somewhere other than your memory of a consultation.

Questions

Florida, answered plainly.

Does a cosmetic surgery office in Florida have to be registered with the state?

Yes, for most cosmetic surgery. An office where a physician performs any Level II or Level III office surgery, or liposuction removing more than 1,000 cubic centimetres of supernatant fat, must register with the Florida Department of Health, unless the facility is already licensed as a hospital or ambulatory surgical centre under chapter 390 or 395. Registered settings must also be inspected by the Department or nationally accredited.

How do I check a Florida surgeon's licence myself?

Use the Department of Health's own licence verification at flhealthsource.gov. Search the exact name on your consent form, then read the status, the profession it covers, and any discipline on the record. Free, and about ten minutes.

What does board certified actually mean here?

On its own, nothing about your procedure. Ask which board certified them and whether that certification covers this operation, then check the certifying board's own lookup rather than the practice's site.

What should I get in writing before paying a deposit?

The operating surgeon by name, the address and its registration status, who gives your anaesthesia and whether they stay, the hospital you would be transferred to, what a revision would cost, and the refund terms on the deposit itself.
