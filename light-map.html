Most of what matters is public. Some of it is kept dark.

License actions, court records, facility accreditation and inspection reports sit in public databases that most patients never open. Peer review, confidential settlements and accreditor survey findings don't.

This map shows you which is which, how to read what you find, and what to ask about the rest. It's here to help you see clearly, not to scare you.

01 — Your surgery and its depth

How deep you go under decides which rules apply.

Facilities are accredited for a maximum depth of anesthesia, often labeled Class A, B or C. An operating room approved for local numbing isn't approved to put you fully under. That match is one of the most overlooked checks in cosmetic surgery.

03 — How to read a record without panicking

Records are context, not verdicts.

Very experienced surgeons usually have something on file. The point isn't to find a spotless record. It's to understand what's there, what isn't, and how the surgeon talks about both.

07 — How the reading works
HALT
Only triggers from a finding: a halt-level record you read as recent, repeated or related; a lapsed license or accreditation; or a halt-level question refused. Dark or not-yet items never trigger it.
HOLD
Stays on while any halt-level item isn't known yet, a finding hasn't been read, the anesthesia depth doesn't match, or you haven't seen the consent form in writing.
ADVANCE
Once every halt-level item is known and nothing says stop. Dark items are still listed so you go ahead knowing what you can't know.
Never
A score, a ranking, a named surgeon, or a reading anyone can pay to change. Records are public; how you read them is up to you.
