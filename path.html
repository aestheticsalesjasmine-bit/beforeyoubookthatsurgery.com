Seroma or hematoma: know the difference
Seroma
Clear to yellow fluid collecting under the skin, often after the drains come out. It can feel like sloshing. It's usually drained in the office with a needle and no anesthesia, and it sometimes needs repeating.
Ask: who drains it once you're home?
Hematoma
Blood collecting under the skin, most often in the first 24–72 hours. It's firm, painful and swollen on one side. It often needs a return to the operating room, which means anesthesia has to be available.
Ask: where, how fast, and who gives the anesthesia?
