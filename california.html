California · State guide

In California, the room has to be accredited before anyone puts you under.

The state settled this in 1996. A physician cannot operate on you in an outpatient setting using anesthesia unless that setting is accredited or licensed, and the Medical Board publishes the list. Four things to check, about twenty minutes, before any deposit.

The setting

An unaccredited outpatient setting is not allowed to put you under.

Section 2216 of the Business and Professions Code has said since July 1996 that a physician and surgeon may not perform procedures in an outpatient setting using anesthesia unless that setting is accredited by a Medical Board approved agency or otherwise licensed. Settings where only anxiolytics and analgesics are given, at doses that do not put your protective reflexes at risk, sit outside the requirement.

You do not have to take the practice's word for it. Health and Safety Code section 1248.2 requires the Medical Board of California to maintain and publish the list of accredited outpatient settings, with each one's accreditation status, on its own site. Start at the Board's outpatient surgery settings page and look up the address on your consent form, not the practice name on the sign.

The hospital

Ask who admits you if it goes wrong, and get the name.

California ties the setting to a hospital on purpose. An accredited outpatient setting may permit surgery only by a licensee who holds admitting privileges at a local accredited or licensed acute care hospital. Where a licensee's professional classification or another administrative limit rules those privileges out, the setting must instead hold a written transfer agreement with someone who has them.

So the question has a right answer on file somewhere. Do you have admitting privileges at a local acute care hospital, and if not, who is the transfer agreement with? A practice that cannot answer that quickly has told you something.

The record

Accredited settings report their bad days, and are reviewed.

Under sections 2216.3 and 2216.4 of the Business and Professions Code, an accredited outpatient setting must report an adverse event to the Medical Board no later than five days after it is detected, and within 24 hours where the event is an ongoing urgent or emergent threat to patients, staff or visitors.

Each licensee operating in a setting that has to be accredited is also peer reviewed at least every two years, covering qualifications, privileges, outcomes and professional conduct, even where the setting has only one licensee. None of that guarantees anything about your operation. It does mean there is a record, and you are allowed to ask what is in it.

The license

Check the license and the board separately, because they are separate things.

Search the exact name printed on your consent form on the Medical Board's consumer license lookup. Read the status, the profession it actually covers, and any discipline on the record.

Then treat board certification as a second question. There is no single body called the board. Ask which board certified them and whether that certification covers the operation you are being offered, then check that board's own lookup rather than the practice's website.

What to get in writing while you still have leverage.

A deposit is the moment your options narrow. Ask for these in writing, in one email, so the answers exist somewhere other than your memory of a consultation.

Questions

California, answered plainly.

Does a California cosmetic surgery office have to be accredited?

If anesthesia is used, generally yes. Business and Professions Code section 2216 provides that a physician and surgeon may not perform procedures in an outpatient setting using anesthesia unless the setting is accredited by a Medical Board approved accreditation agency or is otherwise licensed. Settings giving only anxiolytics and analgesics, at doses that do not risk your protective reflexes, fall outside the requirement.

How do I check whether a California surgery setting is accredited?

Health and Safety Code section 1248.2 requires the Medical Board of California to publish the list of accredited outpatient settings and their accreditation status on its website. Look up the address on your consent form at mbc.ca.gov, under outpatient surgery settings.

Does my California surgeon need hospital admitting privileges?

An accredited outpatient setting may permit surgery only by a licensee with admitting privileges at a local accredited or licensed acute care hospital. Where the licensee is precluded from holding privileges by their professional classification or another administrative limitation, the setting must hold a written transfer agreement with a licensee who has them. Ask which applies and ask for the name.

Are adverse events reported in California?

Yes. Under Business and Professions Code sections 2216.3 and 2216.4, an accredited outpatient setting must report an adverse event to the Medical Board no later than five days after detection, or within 24 hours where the event is an ongoing urgent or emergent threat to patients, personnel or visitors.

Do you recommend California surgeons?

No. This site does not diagnose, assess candidacy, or recommend procedures, surgeons or clinics, and it takes no referral fees. It shows you what can be checked, what still needs asking, and what you want.
